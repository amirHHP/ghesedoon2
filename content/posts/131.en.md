In a little village in the heart of the mountains, there was a big, beautiful garden that made all the villagers proud. The garden was full of colorful flowers, fruit trees, and sweet-smelling plants, and every child who came there was filled with wonder.

One day, the gardeners of the village decided to hold a contest. It was a gardening race, and whoever could keep a garden most beautiful and full of glory would win. Each gardener had to care for the plants, give them water, and look after them until the garden was as lovely as it could be.

Nana Goli and the other gardeners of the village joined the contest with joy and excitement. Nana Goli kept her garden beautiful and fresh, hoping she would be named the best gardener. Every morning she went to her garden early, watered the flowers, trimmed the branches that had grown too long, and tended her plants with care and kindness.

The other gardeners did the same. Each of them worked with energy and hope to keep their own garden well. Some had gardens of red and pink flowers that all shone brightly. Others raced with beautiful fruit trees heavy with fruit.

On the day of the contest, all the gardeners proudly showed their gardens. Nana Goli's garden was full of colorful flowers that amazed everyone. Red, yellow, pink, orange, and purple flowers mixed together, and the garden looked like a magical little patch of earth.

The judges looked at the gardens carefully. They watched each garden closely, touched the flowers and trees, and smelled the scent of the blossoms up close. Then they congratulated all the gardeners and chose Nana Goli's garden as the most beautiful of all.

Nana Goli was happy and proud of her first-place prize. With a smile on her lips, she looked at her garden and said, "This garden never grew so beautiful from dreaming alone. It grew from care and love for the flowers. Today I am glad I can share this beauty with everyone in the village."

With that honor, Nana Goli and the other gardeners of the village celebrated the victory together and enjoyed the beauty and freshness of their gardens.

The End.
